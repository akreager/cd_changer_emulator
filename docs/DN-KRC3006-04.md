## 1. Purpose

This design note documents the requirements, decisions, and constraints for a custom breakout board intended to expose all 13 pins of the Kenwood CD changer interface connector (13-pin mini DIN) to accessible test points and pin headers. The board will be used during protocol capture and analysis phases of TP-KRC3006-01 and will remain part of the test bench for the duration of the reverse engineering effort.

---

## 2. Scope

This document covers the electrical requirements, mechanical considerations, KiCad design notes, fabrication plan, and mockup verification procedure for the breakout board. It does not cover firmware, protocol analysis, or the ATtiny1616/Raspberry Pi integration architecture (see [DN-KRC3006-01 §1](DN-KRC3006-01.md#1-system-architecture-overview)).

---

## 3. Background

The Kenwood KRC-3006 communicates with its companion CD changer via a proprietary synchronous serial protocol carried over a 13-pin mini DIN cable. To capture and analyze this protocol with a logic analyzer, all signal lines must be made accessible in a non-intrusive, electrically safe manner. A purpose-built breakout board provides a clean, repeatable solution and eliminates the need for improvised flying-lead connections that may introduce noise or reliability issues during capture sessions.

## 4. Design Requirements

## 5. Connector Pinout Reference

The factory pin names and resistor values below follow [DN-02 §1, §3.1 and §12](DN-KRC3006-02.md), propagated under approved [rulings R-1/R-2](../.agents/rulings/2026-10-16.md). Directions are relative to the head unit; CLK direction remains open until TP-01 Phase 2C. Changer-side circuit values describe the CX85; the C717 interface has not been analyzed.

#### Pin 1 — /REQ H (CH-REQH) | Head unit → changer
Request handshake. Head-unit series resistor R144: 100 Ω; CX85 R10: 100 Ω (DN-02 §3.1/§12.1).

#### Pin 2 — GND
Ground reference. Phase 1 recorded continuity to pin 6 ([TL-01-P1-20260419](records/TL-KRC3006-01-P1-20260419.pdf), Ground Continuity); its P/F disposition remains pending. Analyzer ground connects only to J3-1 (R-2).

#### Pin 3 — B.U. | Head unit → changer
Battery-unswitched supply, raw battery voltage through the 3 A fuse (DN-02 §2). Keep J3-3 blocked: B.U. can be about 14.4 V, exceeding the analyzer's 5.25 V limit (R-1.1/R-2). Measure at the PSU/DIN connection, never at J3.

#### Pin 4 — ON (CH-CON) | Head unit → changer
Enable signal, head-unit R142: 4.7 kΩ; CX85 R9: 100 Ω (DN-02 §12.3). Polarity remains open: the CX85 manual analysis says active-LOW, but Phase 1 recorded HIGH with CD-CH selected. TP-01 Phase 2A applies R-1.3's connector-level resolution rule.

#### Pin 5 — MUTE (CH-MUTE) | Changer → head unit (CX85 analysis)
Head-unit R140: 7.5 kΩ; CX85 R12: 100 Ω (DN-02 §12.2). DN-02 §16.4 describes active-LOW, while Phase 1 records 0 V idle / 3.5 V enabled. Record CH7 in Phase 2A; do not treat polarity as settled (R-1.3).

#### Pin 6 — D. GND (published alias AGND)
Digital ground. Phase 1 recorded continuity to pin 2; see pin 2 above. Leave analyzer harness position J3-5 empty (R-2).

#### Pin 7 — RESET (CH-RST) | Head unit → changer
Head-unit R141: 1 kΩ; CX85 R11: 100 Ω (DN-02 §12.3). Phase 1 idle 0 V differs from the former HIGH expectation; observe CH6 and the Phase 2B power-on pulse (R-1.4).

#### Pin 8 — R ch | Changer → head unit
Right analog audio, routed through J4/J5 rather than straight through. Tip = DIN pin 8 (R-4; §6).

#### Pin 9 — /REQ C (CH-REQC) | Changer → head unit
Request handshake. CX85 R15: 100 Ω; head-unit R131: 2.2 kΩ (DN-02 §12.2).

#### Pin 10 — DATA C (CH-DATAC) | Changer → head unit
Serial data. CX85 R14: 100 Ω; head-unit R130: 2.2 kΩ (DN-02 §12.2). Phase 1 idle 5.11 V differs from predicted LOW; Phase 2A records CH2 (R-1.3).

#### Pin 11 — DATA H (CH-DATAH) | Head unit → changer
Serial data. Head-unit R143: 100 Ω; CX85 R13: 100 Ω. R145 (100 kΩ) is a pull-down to GND, not a series element (DN-02 §3.1/§12.1). TP-01 §4.2.0 checks the predicted pull-down resistance.

#### Pin 12 — L ch | Changer → head unit
Left analog audio, routed through J4/J5 rather than straight through. Ring = DIN pin 12 (R-4; §6).

#### Pin 13 — /CLK (CLK) | Direction open
Head-unit R129: 2.2 kΩ; CX85 R16: 100 Ω. R146 (100 kΩ) is a pull-down to GND, not a series element (DN-02 §3.1/§12.1). The 125 kHz rate is assumed, unmeasured. Phase 2A measures timing; Phase 2C uses FIX-01 to identify the driver (R-1.3/R-1.5).

## 6. Audio Interception

The audio channels (Pin 8 / R CH and Pin 12 / L CH) are not routed as a direct pass-through between J1 and J2. Instead, they are broken out to two 3.5 mm TRS audio jacks mounted on the right edge of the board:

- **J4** — Head unit side audio. Tip = R CH (head unit), Ring = L CH (head unit), Sleeve = GND.
- **J5** — Changer side audio. Tip = R CH (changer), Ring = L CH (changer), Sleeve = GND.

The audio jacks use a standard 3-conductor (TRS) footprint with no switching contacts. When nothing is plugged in, the audio pins on both the head unit and changer sides float.

This topology enables three operating modes:

| Mode | Configuration | Use Case |
|---|---|---|
| **Audio pass-through** | Connect J4 to J5 with a male-to-male 3.5 mm TRS cable. | Normal protocol capture with authentic changer audio reaching the head unit. Equivalent to a straight pass-through board. |
| **Audio intercept** | Connect monitoring equipment (oscilloscope, audio recorder, powered speakers) to J5. | Listen to or record the changer's audio output without routing it to the head unit. |
| **Audio injection** | Connect an external audio source to J4. Leave J5 disconnected or connected to monitoring equipment. | Feed test tones or music from a phone, DAC, or Raspberry Pi directly into the head unit's audio input path. Useful for verifying the audio path independently of changer protocol state. |

Audio pass-through requires the external cable. The board cannot function as a fully transparent sniffer without it. This is an intentional design decision — the separate jacks provide flexibility that a hardwired pass-through cannot.

## 7. Safety and Operational Procedures

The breakout carries B.U. on DIN pin 3 / J3-3 and nominal 5 V bus signals (DN-02 §2/§3.1). No series resistors are installed in the analyzer leads. Source impedance doesn't limit fault current to a safe value: REQ H and DATA H have 100 Ω on the head-unit side; the CX85 has 100 Ω on every signal. Protection is procedural ([R-2](../.agents/rulings/2026-10-16.md)); the C717's circuitry is not analyzed.

R-2 estimates about 50 mA for a driven HIGH shorted to GND through 100 Ω, about 90 mA into clamp diodes for B.U.-to-signal faults through 100 Ω, and about 2 mA for B.U. shorted to ON through R142 (4.7 kΩ). These are fault estimates, not safe operating ratings.
**The test bench must be powered down before any reconfiguration of breakout wiring, logic analyzer connections, or audio cables.** This is the primary protection against accidental shorts and is a hard requirement — not a recommendation.

Procedure for connecting or reconfiguring the logic analyzer:

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Remove bench power before any wiring change. |
| 2 | Verify B.U. is de-energized at the PSU/DIN connection, never at J3. |
| 3 | Block J3-3 using a heat-shrink-capped cut-off pin or blank plug; leave harness position 3 empty. |
| 4 | Connect the custom crimped analyzer harness to the female J3 socket; ground only at J3-1 and leave J3-5 empty. No series resistors in the analyzer leads. |
| 5 | Tape over exposed joints on the board's back. Connect or rearrange J4/J5 audio cables as needed. |
| 6 | Inspect for bridges, especially B.U. beside ON/RESET. Check the channel map against TP-01 §4.2.1 (Phase 2C changes CH6). |
| 7 | Restore power only after the connections are secure and checked. |

This procedure also applies when swapping between audio operating modes (pass-through, intercept, injection).

## 9. Design Decisions
#### J2 on back side
 The changer-side DIN connector (J2) is mounted on the back (B.Cu) side of the board, opposite J1 on the front. This places J1 and J2 through-hole pins on opposite copper layers with near-1:1 pin alignment, allowing the pass-through traces to be short direct runs rather than routing around one connector to reach the other. This significantly simplifies the layout and reduces trace congestion in the center of the board.
#### No series resistors on J3
 The decision stands under [R-2](../.agents/rulings/2026-10-16.md): the owner accepted the residual risk on 2026-10-16. Corrected source impedances do not provide adequate short protection. The primary protection is power-down reconfiguration, blocking J3-3, grounding only at J3-1, a female J3 socket with a custom crimped harness, and taped rear joints (§7). The owner rejected adding 1 kΩ to each analyzer lead because extra joints among the stiff cables and harness could introduce shorts. Such a resistor would drop only about 1% into the analyzer's >100 kΩ input and would not materially protect its 5.25 V limit (R-1.1/R-2). FIX-01's series Rs is a separate CLK measurement element (§16), not an analyzer-lead protection resistor.
#### Female headers on J3
 Female pin headers (sockets) are used for the test access points. Male jumper wires are inserted to connect the logic analyzer. This approach provides a more secure mechanical connection than clip leads on bare pins and reduces the risk of adjacent-pin bridging.
#### Separated audio path
 Audio channels are routed to 3.5 mm TRS jacks instead of passing through directly. This enables audio intercept and injection modes that support testing the audio path independently of the protocol state. The trade-off is that a male-to-male 3.5 mm cable is required for normal audio pass-through. See Section 6.
#### Non-switched audio jacks
 Standard AudioJack3 footprints (no switching contacts) are used. This simplifies the PCB layout and avoids the failure mode where a worn switching contact intermittently disconnects the audio path. The trade-off is that unplugged jacks leave the audio pins floating rather than defaulting to pass-through.
#### Board outline with cutouts
 The right edge of the board has two cutouts to accommodate the 3.5 mm audio jacks perpendicular to the board edge, keeping the overall width narrow. |

| Step | Action |

## 16. FIX-01 — CLK driver fixture, breakout S/N 2

**Authority:** approved [R-1.5 issue 2 and R-2](../.agents/rulings/2026-10-16.md), owner decision 2026-10-16. PCB geometry is cited there to `96d09e0`. These are build instructions, not evidence that S/N 2 has been modified or tested.

A plain breakout leaves CLK as one node; at 24 MHz its ends switch within one 42 ns sample on bench-length cables. A series resistor Rs separates head-unit and changer nodes so the far cable capacitance delays the receiving node. Falling edges decide direction because the analyzer's bounded threshold produces more lag on falling than rising edges (R-1.5).

### 16.1 Cut and assemble

1. On the **bare board, before assembly**, remove about 0.5 mm of the CLK straight run into J2-13 on **F.Cu**, at x 99.75–100.25 mm, y 131.3 mm (KiCad coordinates), about 1.5 mm from the J2-13 pad edge.
2. DATA H is parallel, 0.52 mm above edge-to-edge; the GND pour is 0.5 mm below. Cut only the front copper: the B.Cu CLK trace to J3-11 runs directly underneath. Do not cut the 45° section or the bus between connectors, where spacing is 0.16 mm.
3. Verify the cut unpowered on the bare board:

| Check | Required result |
|---|---|
| J1-13 ↔ J2-13 | Open |
| J2-13 ↔ J3-11 | <1 Ω |
| J1-11 ↔ J2-11 (DATA H) | <1 Ω |
| J1-10 ↔ J2-10 (DATA C) | <1 Ω |

4. Assemble the board and run TP-02 with CLK recorded as a documented exception. Keep S/N 1's completed TL-02 results separate from S/N 2's execution.

### 16.2 Fit Rs and the CLK-H tap

1. On the back, run insulated wire from the J1-13 joint to a splice between J3-11 and J2's body, clear of that body.
2. Fit sleeved Rs from this splice to the J3-11 joint.
3. Route a short lead from the splice around the board edge to a one-position female housing beside J3 on the front; label it **CLK-H**. Secure the wire and housing so they cannot pull on joints.
4. **J3-11 remains on the changer side of the cut. CLK-H is the head-unit side.** The separate B.Cu connection is J2-13 ↔ J3-11; do not treat J3-11 as a head-unit tap.

### 16.3 Verification and use

With power removed and both cables unplugged, record these checks, the fitted Rs value/tolerance, date and tester:

| Check | Required result |
|---|---|
| J1-13 ↔ CLK-H | <1 Ω |
| CLK-H ↔ J3-11 | Rs within its tolerance |
| J1-13 ↔ J2-13 | Rs within its tolerance |
| CLK-H ↔ J3-1 (GND) | Open |
| CLK-H ↔ J3-10 (DATA H) | Open |

All other signals stay straight through as in the base board; audio retains the external J4–J5 jumper. MOD-03 S/N 1 stays unmodified as the reference. Replacing Rs with a wire link restores S/N 2 to plain pass-through (R-1.5).

Select Rs and execute captures under [TP-01 §4.4](TP-KRC3006-01.md#44-phase-2c-clk-driver-identification): 1 kΩ, 2.2 kΩ, 4.7 kΩ or 10 kΩ maximum, targeting falling-edge lag ≥2 samples (83 ns at 24 MHz) and ≤10% of the measured CLK half-period. Record both cable capacitances if measured, and confirm normal C717 communication at every value; step down and record any failure. At 24 MHz, CH0 = J3-11 (changer) and CH6 = CLK-H (head unit); RESET is not captured in Phase 2C.

The build's TP-02 execution and exception need their own S/N 2 working record; the completed S/N 1 record must not be reused or overwritten. Record publication waits for the actual build and owner completion of its test log.
