### R-1.5 New Phase 2C: CLK driver identification (Owner decision 2026-10-16: build FIX-01 from breakout S/N 2)

**Why a fixture is needed.** On the unmodified breakout, CLK is a single node. On bench-length cables an edge reaches every point on it well inside one 42 ns sample, so both ends switch on the same sample whichever side drives, and a logic analyzer can't tell the driver. DN-02 §16.2 asks for the driver without giving a method.

A series resistor Rs splits the line into a head-unit node and a changer node. The driver's node switches first. The far node has to charge or discharge its own capacitance (mostly its cable) through Rs, so it switches later.

**Size of the lag.** It's about Rs × C_far × k, where C_far is the far node's capacitance and k depends on where the analyzer's threshold sits in the ~5.1 V swing. The SparkFun spec only bounds the threshold between 0.8 and 2.0 V (R-1.1), so:

| Edge | k | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Falling | ln(5.1 / Vth) ≈ 0.9–1.9 | Large lag. This edge decides the direction. |
| Rising | ln(5.1 / (5.1 − Vth)) ≈ 0.2–0.5 | Small lag. It may show as a tie. |

Issue 1 used Rs·C·ln 2, which assumes a mid-rail threshold.

**Fixture FIX-01 (breakout S/N 2).** Source: the KiCad PCB at `96d09e0`.
- CLK runs J1-13 → J2-13 on F.Cu.
- J3-11 is tied to J2-13 by a separate B.Cu trace.

So any cut between J1 and J2 leaves J3-11 on the **changer** side, and the head-unit side needs a new tap.

1. **Cut**, on the front (F.Cu), on the bare board before assembly:
   - Remove about 0.5 mm of CLK in its straight run into J2-13, at x 99.75–100.25 mm, y 131.3 mm (KiCad coordinates). That's about 1.5 mm from the edge of J2-13's pad.
   - DATA H runs parallel, 0.52 mm above (edge to edge). The GND pour is 0.5 mm below.
   - Cut only the copper. The B.Cu CLK trace to J3-11 runs directly underneath.
   - Don't cut the 45° section or the bus between the connectors: their spacing is 0.16 mm.
2. **Verify the cut** on the bare board:
   - J1-13 ↔ J2-13: open
   - J2-13 ↔ J3-11: < 1 Ω
   - J1-11 ↔ J2-11 (DATA H) and J1-10 ↔ J2-10 (DATA C): < 1 Ω
3. **Assemble the board, and run TP-02** with CLK as a documented exception.
4. **Fit Rs**, on the back:
   - Run an insulated wire from the J1-13 joint to a splice between J3-11 and J2's body, clear of the body.
   - Fit Rs, sleeved, from the splice to the J3-11 joint.
   - Take a short lead from the splice round the board edge to a 1-position female housing beside J3 on the front. Label it CLK-H.
   - Secure the wire and the housing so they can't pull on the joints.
5. **Verify FIX-01**, unpowered, with both cables unplugged:
   - J1-13 ↔ CLK-H: < 1 Ω
   - CLK-H ↔ J3-11 and J1-13 ↔ J2-13: Rs, within its tolerance
   - CLK-H ↔ J3-1 (GND) and CLK-H ↔ J3-10 (DATA H): open

Every other signal stays straight through. MOD-03 S/N 1 stays unmodified as the reference fixture. Replacing Rs with a wire link returns S/N 2 to plain pass-through.

**Probes (Phase 2C only):**
- CH0 = J3-11, the changer side.
- CH6 = CLK-H, the head-unit side. RESET isn't needed, because 2C has no power cycle.
- Every other channel is as in R-1.1.

**Choosing Rs.** Rs delays CLK, but not DATA H or DATA C, at the far end, so it skews the clock against the data. The target is a falling-edge lag of at least 2 samples (83 ns at 24 MHz), and no more than 10 % of the CLK half-period measured in Phase 2A.
- **If the DMM measures capacitance:** measure pin 13 ↔ pin 2 on each cable, unplugged at both ends. Set Rs ≈ 150 ns ÷ the larger of the two, rounded to 1 k, 2.2 k, 4.7 k or 10 kΩ. Record both values. If they differ by more than 2×, the lag on the smaller side may be under 2 samples. That only matters if the changer turns out to drive.
- **Otherwise:** start at 1 kΩ, and step to 2.2 k, 4.7 k, then 10 kΩ until the falling-edge lag reaches 2 samples. Cable capacitance is unknown, and a multi-metre changer cable could make Issue 1's 4.7 kΩ start too slow.
- **Ceiling: 10 kΩ.** With the head unit's 100 kΩ pull-down on CLK (R146), a changer-driven HIGH at the head-unit side would still be about 4.4 V.
- **Precondition at every value:** the C717 plays with no error codes and a normal display. If it doesn't, step down and record that.

**Procedure:**
- Capture Phase 2B's steady-state and track-change sequences through FIX-01 at 24 MHz.
- For each frame type, record:
  - the leading side
  - the lag, in samples and in ns
  - whether /REQ H or /REQ C started the transfer

**Resolution rule.** Falling edges decide.
- **Head-unit-driven only:** CH6 leads by at least 2 samples on every falling CLK edge in every frame type, and CH0 leads on no rising edge. The SN74LVC8T245 arrangement stands.
- **Bidirectional:** in some frame types, CH0 leads falling edges by at least 2 samples. That triggers the SN74LVC1T45 fallback for CLK (DN-01).
- **Anything else** is an incident. That includes ties on falling edges, and a leading side that changes within one frame.

**Ordering:**
- Entry: Phase 2B passed, and FIX-01 built and verified.
- 2C doesn't gate Phase 3. It gates the HAT's CLK level-shifter choice.
- If an oscilloscope is available, a two-channel scope across Rs on the same fixture gives the same answer directly.

## R-2 · DC-07 — Breakout protection under the corrected impedances

**Finding.** DN-04 §7 and §9 justify having no series resistors on J3 by "100K+ on CLK, DATA H and REQ H". On the corrected values (DN-02 Rev C §12), the worst cases are:

| Fault at J3 | Line | Series resistance limiting it | Fault current |
|---|---|---|---|
| A HIGH line shorted to GND | REQ H, DATA H (driven by the head unit) | 100 Ω (R144, R143) | ≈ 50 mA |
| A HIGH line shorted to GND | Lines the changer drives | 100 Ω in the CX85 (R9–R16); the C717 isn't analyzed | ≈ 50 mA |
| B.U. (~14.4 V) shorted to a signal | REQ H, DATA H into the head unit; any line into the changer | 100 Ω | ≈ 90 mA into the IC's clamp diodes |
| B.U. shorted to ON | ON | 4.7 kΩ (R142) | ≈ 2 mA |

The impedance doesn't protect anything. The procedure is the only protection. On J3, B.U. (pin 3) sits between ON (pin 2) and RESET (pin 4), and D. GND (pin 5) sits next to /REQ H (pin 6).

**Ruling.** The board is acceptable as built, with the precautions below. **Owner decision (2026-10-16):** the residual risk is accepted.

1. **Keep the power-down rule** (DN-04 §7). It's the primary protection, unchanged.
2. **Block J3-3.** Leave position 3 empty in the harness, and fit a cut-off pin capped with heat-shrink, or a single blank plug, so nothing can be inserted there. B.U. would also exceed the analyzer's 5.25 V input limit (R-1.1) about three times over. Measure B.U. with the DMM at the PSU or the DIN shell, never at J3.
3. **Ground only at J3-1,** and leave J3-5 empty. That removes the GND-next-to-/REQ H bridge.
4. **No series resistors in the analyzer leads** (owner decision, 2026-10-16). Issue 1 proposed 1 kΩ in each lead. The owner rejected it: joints at the board end, between the stiff changer cables and the analyzer harness, would add short risk of their own. His protection instead:
   - J3 is a female socket.
   - The analyzer connects through a custom crimped harness.
   - The exposed joints on the back of the board are taped over.

   A series resistor wouldn't have added margin against the 5.25 V input limit anyway. Into more than 100 kΩ of analyzer input, 1 kΩ drops about 1 %.

**DN-04 Rev D text** (document control applies it):
- §5 per-pin resistances: rewrite from DN-02 Rev C §3.1/§12, giving the CX85 changer-side values as R10, R13 and R16 at 100 Ω.
- §7: replace the impedance sentence with "Source impedance doesn't limit fault current: 100 Ω on REQ H and DATA H, and 100 Ω on the CX85's side of every line. Protection is procedural." Add precautions 2 and 3, and the owner's measures from item 4, to the procedure table.
- §9 "No series resistors on J3": keep the decision. Replace the rationale with this ruling's, and record the owner's risk acceptance (2026-10-16).
- New section: FIX-01, the modification of S/N 2 (R-1.5): the cut location, Rs, the CLK-H tap, and the verification checks.

## R-3 · DC-24 — ATtiny supply voltage and direct drive (DN-02 §3.1, §5.2, §7.7, §16.6)

**Finding.** DN-02 recommends things the later DN-01 decisions superseded:
- §5.2 and §7.7 recommend running the ATtiny1616 at 5 V, with a voltage divider on its UART TX to the Pi.
- §3.1 ("Implication for emulator") says the ATtiny's own GPIO drives the bus directly.
- §16.6 assumes "5V for Pi/ATtiny, separate 3.3V LDO for DAC".

The settled design is different (DN-01; technical baseline):
- The ATtiny runs at 3.3 V.
- The bus is driven through two SN74LVC8T245, whose B side is on 5 V.
- The UART to the Pi is direct at 3.3 V, with no divider.

**Ruling.** DN-02's *head-unit facts* stand: the µ-COM runs at 5.0–5.1 V, and the bus is 5 V logic. Those facts are why the level shifters exist. Its *emulator recommendations* in these four places are historical. Don't rewrite them; mark them.

- **§5.2 and §7.7:** add "Superseded by DN-01: the ATtiny1616 runs at 3.3 V and drives the bus through SN74LVC8T245 level shifters (B side 5 V), and the UART to the Pi is direct at 3.3 V. The 3.3 V-HIGH-through-2.2 kΩ concern doesn't arise, because the bus side is driven at 5 V." Strike the divider recommendation.
- **§3.1 "Implication for emulator":** add "The emulator drives the bus through the level shifters, not ATtiny GPIO (DN-01)." The edge-rate conclusion still holds.
- **§16.6:** keep the CX85 rail observation as context. Replace the "two-rail approach" sentence with "The HAT's supply rails are DN-01's decision; the power section isn't drawn yet (technical baseline)." Keep the LC-filter suggestion as an open recommendation for DN-01's power design.

This is a **P** change: it applies decisions already settled in DN-01. Add a Rev D history row to DN-02.

## R-6 · DC-21 — How the UART baud rate gets selected (D-12)

**Review of TP-03 §4.3.2.** The plan picks "9600 (sufficient bandwidth…; reliable at 3.3 V with long breadboard wires)".

- **Bandwidth isn't the constraint.** At ~1 message/s of ~25 bytes (`STATE:PLAY:D:TT:MM:SS\n`), every candidate rate has far more capacity than needed.
- **Wire length isn't a real constraint either,** at ≤115.2 kbit/s (8.7 µs per bit) with push-pull drivers.
- **What actually decides reliability:**
  - the frame-timing error between the ATtiny's internal oscillator and the Pi's UART, across the vehicle's temperature range `[TBD — source needed: ATtiny1616 oscillator accuracy from its datasheet]`
  - command latency: the time from a head-unit button press to the Pi acting
  - a parse/checksum error rate measured on real hardware

**Ruling.** Replace the baud sentence in §4.3.2 with: "Baud rate: not specified (D-12). Select it in §4.3.2a."

**New TP-03 §4.3.2a "UART rate selection"** goes before step 3.

1. Candidate rates are 9600, 38400 and 115200.
2. For each rate, on the actual wiring:
   - **Bit-time error:** have the ATtiny transmit `0x55` continuously. On the logic analyzer that's a square wave at baud/2. Measure its frequency and compute the error in %.
   - **Soak:** exchange 10,000 STATE/command message pairs. Count framing errors and parse errors.
   - **Latency:** measure from a head-unit button press (the /REQ H edge on the bus) to the Pi's playback reaction (command logged), over 20 presses. Record the maximum.
   - If you can, repeat the bit-time error measurement cold (< 10 °C) and warm (> 40 °C).
3. **Selection:** the lowest rate with zero errors in the soak, bit-time error under a margin the architect sets once measured, and maximum latency under a limit the owner sets. Record the choice and its rationale in TL-03, then update DN-01 and DN-05 (DC-21).

**TL-03 change.** In "Phase 3 — Integration", add a "UART Rate Selection" table with columns Rate, Bit-time error (%), Temp (°C), Messages, Framing errors, Parse errors, Max latency (ms), P/F, Notes, and a "Selected rate / rationale" row.

Recommendation: the line protocol should carry a checksum, so that "parse errors" can be measured. That's a design decision for DN-01; until then, count malformed lines.
